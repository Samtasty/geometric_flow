1. Objective

Learn a better item-assignment policy from logged student interaction data using off-policy evaluation (OPE). Given a student's current proficiency θ and the round they are on, we want to find a policy π(δ | θ) over item difficulty δ that maximises expected reward:

reward = correct × (δ − δmin)

This encourages assigning harder items that students can answer correctly.

IRT Model

P(correct | θ, δ) = sigmoid(θ − δ)

Proficiency θ and difficulty δ are estimated by the KTM pipeline prior to these experiments.

Students are split 80% / 10% / 10% at the user level (not row level), ensuring no data leakage across splits. All rows for a given student land in exactly one split.

3. Behavior Policy Fitting

For each dataset and each round k, a truncated Gaussian behavior policy is fit on the train split:

πb(δ | θ, k) = TruncNormal(μk(θ), σk(θ)²; [δmin − ε, δmax + ε])
  • μk(θ) = βμ,0 + βμ,1·θ (degree-1 polynomial)
  • log σk(θ) = βσ,0 + βσ,1·θ + βσ,2·θ² (degree-2 polynomial)
  • Support expanded by ε = 1×10−4 to avoid float32 boundary artifacts
  • Fitted via L-BFGS-B minimising the truncated Gaussian negative log-likelihood

Auto-Exclusion of Poorly-Fitted Rounds

Rounds with RMSE > 5.0 are automatically excluded from training data and MIS estimation. This catches degenerate optimizer solutions (μ ≪ δmin with huge σ → near-uniform distribution).

4. Off-Policy Reward & Propensity

Reward

ri = correcti × (δi − δmin)

δmin is the global minimum difficulty across the full dataset.

Propensity

log πb(δi | θi, ki) = log TruncNormal(δi; μk(θi), σk(θi))

Computed using log_ndtr (scipy) with survival-function branch switching for numerical stability:

  • For α > 0: log Z = log[Φ(−α) − Φ(−β)] (SF branch)
  • For α ≤ 0: log Z = log[Φ(β) − Φ(α)] (CDF branch)

This matches exactly the implementation in GlobalGaussianPolicy.log_prob (PyTorch, float64 precision), ensuring IS weights average to ≈ 1 for the behavior policy.

5. Context Reweighting

All estimators target the last round's θ distribution. A histogram density ratio is computed per sample:

ci = qtarget(θi) / qround(i)(θi)

where qtarget is the empirical θ histogram of a target round selected from the first half of rounds by minimum Jensen-Shannon divergence to the last round. Ratios are clipped at 20 and normalized to mean 1.

6. Learned Policy

A single GlobalGaussianPolicy is learned (not per-round), parameterized by:

πnew(δ | θ) = TruncNormal(μ(θ), σ(θ)²; [δmin, δmax])

with the same polynomial parameterization as the behavior policy (degree 1 for μ, degree 2 for σ).

7. Estimators

All estimators evaluate policy value V(π) on the test split, with context weights ci and importance weights wi = πnew(δi|θi) / πb(δi|θi).

10. Key Technical Notes (v2 Fixes)

Truncated Gaussian Support Expansion

δmin and δmax are expanded by 1×10−4 before fitting and policy evaluation. This prevents float32 boundary rejection: observations exactly at δmin in float64 become slightly outside support after float32 round-trip inside GlobalGaussianPolicy.log_prob.

scipy / PyTorch Propensity Alignment

propensity.py now uses log_ndtr with the same survival-function branch switching as GlobalGaussianPolicy.log_prob. This ensures behavior-policy IS weights average to ≈ 1.0 (previously showed min = −23.56, std = 1.71 in log-weight space).

Degenerate Behavior Policy Detection

Rounds where L-BFGS-B finds μ ≪ δmin with huge σ (near-uniform approximation of a bimodal/boundary-spiked distribution) are detected via RMSE > 5.0 and excluded from data and MIS mixture. Root cause: bimodal difficulty distributions with a spike at δmin, growing from ~9% at round 5 to ~20% at round 7 in the attempts dataset.
